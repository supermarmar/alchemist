## Definition

Pillar 1 sets the minimum amount of regulatory capital a bank must hold against
credit, market, and operational risk, measured as a ratio to its risk-weighted
assets.

## The expression

$$
\frac{C}{\mathrm{RWA}} \ge 8\%
$$

Here $C$ is the bank's eligible own funds and $\mathrm{RWA}$ is its total
risk-weighted assets, summed across credit, market, and operational risk; the
European Union's Capital Requirements Regulation further splits this eight per
cent minimum into four and a half per cent common equity tier one, six per cent
tier one, and eight per cent total capital.

## Why this node exists

Without a ratio measured against risk-weighted assets rather than total assets,
two banks of identical size but different risk profiles would face the same
capital charge, precisely the flaw the 1988 Basel Accord's flat five-bucket
risk-weight architecture left unresolved. The Pillar 1 minimum capital
requirement amount needs this node next, translating the ratio into the
capital figure a bank must actually hold.
