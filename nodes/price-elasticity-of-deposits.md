## Definition

The price elasticity of deposits measures how sensitive the volume of deposits a
bank attracts, or retains, is to a change in the rate it offers, expressed as the
ratio of the proportional change in deposit volume to the proportional change in
rate that caused it.

## The expression

$$
\varepsilon_D = \frac{\%\Delta Q_D}{\%\Delta i_D}
$$

Here $Q_D$ is the volume of deposits held, $i_D$ is the rate the bank offers on
them, and $\varepsilon_D$ is the resulting elasticity, negative for a typical rate
cut that drives deposits away and positive for a rate rise that attracts them.

## Why this node exists

A bank setting its deposit rate is choosing a point on the trade-off between funding
cost and funding volume, and that trade-off is only quantifiable once the
sensitivity of volume to rate is known, since a low elasticity lets the bank cut the
rate with little loss of funding while a high elasticity punishes the same cut
severely.
