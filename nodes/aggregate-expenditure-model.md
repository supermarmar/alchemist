## Definition

The aggregate expenditure model finds the equilibrium level of national income as the
output at which planned spending by households, firms, government and overseas buyers
exactly equals that output; it is degenerate at any output level for which planned
spending would fall short, since firms respond by cutting production and do not let
inventories run down indefinitely.

## The expression

$$
Y^{*} = C(Y^{*}) + I + G + (X - M)
$$

Here $Y^{*}$ is the equilibrium level of national income, $C(Y^{*})$ is consumption
spending as a function of that income, $I$ is planned investment, $G$ is government
spending, $X$ is exports and $M$ is imports. Equilibrium is the fixed point at which
income equals the aggregate expenditure that income itself generates.

## Why this node exists

Output does not settle at whatever level firms happen to produce; it settles only where
planned spending matches it, and the aggregate expenditure model is what fixes that level
rather than leaving it undetermined. The Keynesian multiplier needs it next, since the
multiplier measures how much $Y^{*}$ moves when one of the spending components on its
right-hand side shifts.
