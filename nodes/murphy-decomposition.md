Murphy's decomposition splits the expected value of a strictly consistent scoring function into uncertainty, which belongs to the data alone, discrimination, which a model earns by separating observations, and miscalibration, which it gives back by mispricing the cohorts it has separated. On a squared-error score the three terms are the reliability, resolution and uncertainty of the classical Brier score decomposition.
