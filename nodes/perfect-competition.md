## Definition

Perfect competition is the market structure in which many small firms sell an
identical product and no single firm can influence the price, so each firm takes the
market price as given and chooses only how much to supply at it.

## The expression

$$
P = MC(Q^*)
$$

Here $P$ is the market price, which a price-taking firm also faces as its marginal
revenue, $MC$ is the firm's marginal cost, and $Q^*$ is the output at which the firm
maximises profit by equating the two.

## Why this node exists

A monopolist restricts output to hold price above marginal cost, and perfect
competition is the benchmark against which that restriction is measured, since
here price is driven down to marginal cost by the threat of entry alone. Market
failure needs it next, since every source of market failure is defined by the way it
breaks one of the conditions this benchmark assumes.
