## Definition

Capital structure is a company's choice between debt and equity finance, and dividend policy is
its choice of how much of a period's profit to retain against how much to pay out to
shareholders.

## The expression

$$
g = \frac{D}{D+E}, \qquad p = \frac{\mathrm{Div}}{\mathrm{PAT}}
$$

Here $g$ is the gearing ratio, $D$ is the market or book value of debt and $E$ the value of
equity, so $g$ measures how much of the company's financing is debt-funded. Separately, $p$ is
the payout ratio, $\mathrm{Div}$ is the dividend declared for the period, and $\mathrm{PAT}$ is
profit after tax, so $p$ measures how much of that period's profit shareholders receive.

## Why this node exists

A company's choice of gearing changes the risk borne by its equity holders even before any
dividend is paid, since debt claims are serviced ahead of equity, and its dividend policy then
decides how much of what remains after that risk is passed on to shareholders immediately, and how much the company retains. Cost of capital, the prerequisite this node builds on, only has a single number to
report once the balance between debt and equity, and the payout that balance supports, has
actually been fixed.
