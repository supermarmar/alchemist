A confounder is a common cause of both a covariate and a response, so a marginal association between the two carries an open path running through the confounder rather than a signal isolated to the covariate itself. Conditioning on the confounder blocks that path, and its absence is a common explanation for a marginal effect that reverses sign once a relevant grouping variable is held fixed.
