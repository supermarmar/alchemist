## Definition

Net interest spread expresses a bank's interest-rate performance as the gap between the
average yield it earns on interest-earning assets and the average rate it pays on its
interest-bearing liabilities, standing alongside net interest margin as the second of the two
ratio measures built on net interest income.

## The expression

$$
\text{Spread} = r_A - r_L
$$

Here $r_A$ is the average yield earned on interest-earning assets over the period and $r_L$ is
the average rate paid on interest-bearing liabilities over the same period. Spread and net
interest margin usually move together and are often quoted side by side, but they can diverge
where a bank funds part of its earning assets with non-interest-bearing liabilities, such as
current account balances paying no interest, which raises the margin without raising the
spread.

## Why this node exists

A margin measured against total earning assets blends the pure rate difference with the effect
of how much of the balance sheet is funded for free, whereas
a spread measured purely as a rate difference isolates that pricing gap on its own. Hence,
reporting both side by side is what lets a divergence between them be read as a signal about
the funding mix. Net interest income over the economic cycle needs this node next, tracking how
both the spread and the margin move as the cycle turns.
