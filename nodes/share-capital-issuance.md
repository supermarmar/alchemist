## Definition

Share capital issuance is the process by which a company raises equity finance by issuing new
shares, recorded in its accounts at the shares' nominal value, with any amount received above
that value credited separately as share premium.

## The expression

$$
P = (p - n) \times q
$$

Here $P$ is the share premium arising on the issue, $p$ is the price at which the shares are
issued, $n$ is their nominal value, and $q$ is the number of shares issued. The nominal value
times $q$ is credited to share capital, and $P$ is credited to the share premium account.

## Why this node exists

A company issuing shares above their nominal value must record the excess separately, since
company law restricts what a share premium account can be used for and treats it differently
from called-up share capital. Without that split, a set of accounts could not show how much of
a company's equity is capital protected against distribution and how much is a reserve
available for other purposes, and that boundary is what every subsequent capital-maintenance
and dividend decision has to respect.
