## Definition

The risk-return trade-off states that an asset's expected return rises with its
exposure to the risk that cannot be diversified away, so an organisation cannot
raise its expected return without accepting more of that risk. Diversifiable risk
carries no compensation, since it can be removed by holding a broader portfolio at
no cost to expected return.

## The expression

$$
E(R) = R_f + \beta \bigl(E(R_m) - R_f\bigr)
$$

Here $E(R)$ is the asset's expected return, $R_f$ is the risk-free rate, $E(R_m)$
is the expected return on the market portfolio, and $\beta$ measures the asset's
sensitivity to market movements, its exposure to non-diversifiable risk. The term
$E(R_m) - R_f$ is the market risk premium, and an asset earns a multiple of it set
by its own $\beta$.

## Why this node exists

An organisation weighing whether to take on more risk needs to know what expected
return that risk should command before it can judge whether the trade is worth
making. Without this relationship, a shift in risk profile could be assessed only
by its cost, never against the compensation it should bring, leaving the board no
basis for deciding whether the shift raises or destroys value.
