## Definition

A monopoly is the sole supplier in its market, sustained by a barrier that keeps
rivals out. It sets output where marginal revenue equals marginal cost and reads the
corresponding price off the demand curve.

## The expression

$$
MR(Q^*) = MC(Q^*)
$$

Here $Q^*$ is the monopolist's profit-maximising output, $MR$ is marginal revenue,
the change in total revenue from selling one more unit, and $MC$ is marginal cost,
the change in total cost from producing it.

## Why this node exists

A price-taking firm equates price to marginal cost, but a monopolist faces a
downward-sloping demand curve, so marginal revenue falls below price and the
profit-maximising output falls below the competitive level at a higher price.
Barriers to entry and contestability needs this condition next, since what keeps a
monopoly's price above marginal cost is precisely what stops a rival from entering
and competing it away.
