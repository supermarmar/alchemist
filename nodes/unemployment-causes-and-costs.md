## Definition

Unemployment has several distinct causes, from a general shortfall in aggregate demand to
workers moving voluntarily between jobs, and it imposes costs on the unemployed themselves and
on the level of economic activity more widely.

## The expression

$$
u = \frac{U}{L}
$$

Here $U$ is the number of people who are unemployed, meaning without work but actively seeking
and available for it, $L$ is the labour force, the sum of the employed and the unemployed, and
$u$ is the unemployment rate that governments, central banks and forecasters use to compare
labour markets across time and across countries.

## Why this node exists

Distinguishing frictional, structural and demand-deficient unemployment matters because each
calls for a different policy response, and none of that distinction is visible in the single
aggregate rate $u$ on its own. The Phillips curve needs this rate next, since it relates
$u$ to the rate of wage or price inflation and is the mechanism by which a change in
unemployment is expected to feed through to the wider economy.
