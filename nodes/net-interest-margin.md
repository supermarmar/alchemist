## Definition

The net interest margin expresses a bank's net interest income as a proportion of its earning
assets, so that banks of different sizes can be compared on the same footing.

## The expression

$$
\text{NIM} = \frac{\text{NII}}{\bar{A}_e}
$$

Here NIM is the net interest margin, NII is net interest income over the period, and
$\bar{A}_e$ is the average balance of earning assets held over the same period. A NIM that
narrows while lending volumes hold steady points to compressed spreads between lending and
funding rates, whereas a NIM that narrows alongside falling volumes points to weaker demand for
credit as well.

## Why this node exists

A currency amount of net interest income says nothing about how efficiently a bank is
deploying its balance sheet, since a larger book will naturally earn more in absolute terms
without earning it any more efficiently. Expressing that income as a margin removes the size
effect and leaves the rate at which the balance sheet actually earns. Low interest rate
strategic impact needs this node next, since a compressed policy rate squeezes NIM directly and
a bank's strategic response is judged against the margin it protects.
