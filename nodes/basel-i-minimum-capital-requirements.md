## Definition

The 1988 Basel Accord set a minimum ratio of a bank's capital to its risk-weighted
assets, with total capital required to reach at least 8 per cent and Tier 1 capital
alone required to reach at least 4 per cent of that same base.

## The expression

$$
\frac{\text{Total capital}}{\text{RWA}} \ge 8\%
$$

Here total capital is the sum of the bank's Tier 1 and Tier 2 capital, and $\mathrm{RWA}$
is risk-weighted assets, each on-balance-sheet exposure multiplied by one of five fixed
risk weights, zero, 10, 20, 50 or 100 per cent, according to the broad category of
counterparty it falls into.

## Why this node exists

Before 1988 a bank's capital adequacy was judged against total assets or left to national
discretion, and expressing the requirement as a ratio to risk-weighted assets, calibrated
internationally rather than unilaterally, is what let supervisors in different
jurisdictions apply a comparable standard for the first time. Shortcomings of the Basel
Accord needs it next, since the crude five-bucket risk weighting this node fixes is
exactly what later reform set out to refine.
