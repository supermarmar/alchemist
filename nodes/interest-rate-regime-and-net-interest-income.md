## Definition

A bank's net interest income moves with a change in interest rates according to
the gap between the amount of interest-earning assets and interest-bearing
liabilities that reprice within a given horizon, known as the repricing gap.

## The expression

$$
\Delta \mathrm{NII} \approx \mathrm{GAP} \times \Delta r, \qquad
\mathrm{GAP} = \mathrm{RSA} - \mathrm{RSL}
$$

Here $\mathrm{RSA}$ is the amount of rate-sensitive assets repricing within the
horizon, $\mathrm{RSL}$ is the amount of rate-sensitive liabilities repricing
within the same horizon, $\mathrm{GAP}$ is their difference, and $\Delta r$ is the
change in interest rates applied uniformly across the repricing book.

## Why this node exists

A bank whose assets reprice faster than its liabilities carries a positive gap
and gains net interest income when rates rise, while a bank funded short against
long fixed-rate lending carries a negative gap and loses income in the same
scenario. Whether a change in the interest rate regime helps or hurts a bank's
earnings therefore depends on the sign and size of this gap as much as on the
direction of the rate move.
