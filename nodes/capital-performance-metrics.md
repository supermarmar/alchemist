## Definition

Return on capital measures how well a business line is using the capital allocated to it,
setting the profit that line generates against the capital it has been assigned, so a line
consuming little capital and a line consuming much can be compared on one scale.

## The expression

$$
\mathrm{RAROC} = \frac{\text{Revenue} - \text{Costs} - \text{Expected loss}}{\text{Economic capital}}
$$

Here revenue and costs are the business line's income and expenses over the period, expected
loss is the average loss the line's credit or insurance risk is expected to generate, and
economic capital is the capital the line has been allocated against its unexpected loss.
Risk-adjusted return on capital, RAROC, therefore measures profitability net of the risk that
consumed the capital in the first place. Profitability alone cannot show that.

## Why this node exists

A business line that reports strong profit but consumes a disproportionate share of the bank's
capital may be destroying value once that capital is priced in, and revenue or profit alone
cannot show that. Setting return against the capital a line actually uses lets a bank compare
lines of very different size and risk on one common scale, which is the comparison capital
allocation across the bank ultimately rests on.
