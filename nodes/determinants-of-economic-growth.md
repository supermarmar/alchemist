## Definition

An economy's rate of output growth decomposes into the growth of the productivity with which its
capital and labour are combined and the growth of those two inputs themselves, each weighted by
the share of income it commands.

## The expression

$$
g_Y = g_A + \alpha g_K + (1-\alpha) g_L
$$

Here $g_Y$ is output growth, $g_A$ is total factor productivity growth, capturing every source
of growth not explained by more capital or more labour, $g_K$ is capital growth, $g_L$ is labour
growth, and $\alpha$ is capital's share of national income, so that $1-\alpha$ is labour's share.

## Why this node exists

A growth rate reported on its own says nothing about whether an economy is growing because it is
accumulating more capital, employing more people, or becoming more productive with what it
already has, and each of those three sources carries a different policy implication. Where
capital and labour growth are already near their sustainable limits, only productivity growth
can lift potential output further, so this decomposition is what turns a single growth figure
into a diagnosis of where an economy's growth is actually coming from.
