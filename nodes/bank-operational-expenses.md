## Definition

Operational expenses are the staff, premises, technology and other running costs of
operating a bank, kept distinct in its accounts from credit losses and from the cost of
funding.

## The expression

$$
\text{cost-income ratio} = \frac{\text{operating expenses}}{\text{operating income}}
$$

Here operating expenses is the total running cost over the period and operating income is
the revenue the bank generates from its activities before credit losses are charged, so the
cost-income ratio states how much of every unit of income operating costs consume. A lower
ratio leaves more income available to absorb credit losses and to remunerate capital.

## Why this node exists

A bank can hold sound assets and still report a weak return once its running costs are set
against its income, so operational efficiency is judged separately from credit quality
even though both feed the same bottom line. The cost-income ratio is the figure that
separates the two, letting a lender at higher risk with lower costs be compared fairly
against one running the reverse.
