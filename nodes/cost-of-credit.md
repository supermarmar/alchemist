## Definition

The cost of credit is the charge a bank recognises against income for the credit
losses its lending book generates, measured relative to the size of the book so that
portfolios of different sizes can be compared.

## The expression

$$
\text{CoR} = \frac{\text{Impairment charge}}{\text{Average gross loans}}
$$

Here $\text{CoR}$ is the cost of risk, the impairment charge is the expense a bank
books in a period for expected and incurred credit losses, and the average gross loans
is the average balance of the lending book that charge is measured against over that
period.

## Why this node exists

A bank cannot compare the credit performance of a small specialist book against a
large mainstream one from the impairment charge alone, since the larger book shows a
larger charge even where it is better underwritten. Expressing the charge as a ratio
to the book it was raised against is what makes that comparison meaningful, and the
same ratio disciplines how a bank prices new lending against the losses it expects to
incur.
