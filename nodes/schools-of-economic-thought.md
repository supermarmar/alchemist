Economic schools of thought, including classical, Marxian, neo-classical, Keynesian, monetarist and Austrian, differ in their assumptions about how markets clear, how much government should intervene and what drives growth and unemployment. Comparing them explains why economists can reach different policy conclusions from the same data.
