## Definition

A firm's revenue depends on the demand it faces: average revenue is price itself, and marginal
revenue is the extra revenue earned from selling one more unit, which falls short of price
wherever the firm must cut price on every unit to sell more. A firm maximises profit at the
output where marginal revenue equals marginal cost, provided that output also covers variable
cost.

## The expression

$$
\pi(Q) = TR(Q) - TC(Q), \qquad \frac{d\pi}{dQ} = MR(Q) - MC(Q) = 0 \implies MR(Q^*) = MC(Q^*)
$$

Here $\pi(Q)$ is profit at output $Q$, $TR(Q)$ and $TC(Q)$ are total revenue and total cost, and
$MR(Q)$ and $MC(Q)$ are their derivatives with respect to $Q$, marginal revenue and marginal
cost. Setting the derivative of profit to zero gives the profit-maximising output $Q^*$, the
point at which marginal revenue and marginal cost are equal.

## Why this node exists

A firm facing a downward-sloping demand curve earns less on the marginal unit than the average
unit already sold, so its profit-maximising rule cannot simply be price equals marginal cost, the
rule that holds only for a price-taking firm. This node supplies the general rule that applies
whatever market structure the firm sells into, and monopoly needs it next, since a monopolist's
pricing decision is this same equality applied to a demand curve the firm alone faces.
