## Definition

The shutdown point is the price at which a firm's revenue exactly covers its variable costs of production. Below this price, producing anything at all adds a loss on top of the fixed costs already sunk, so output falls to zero.

## The expression

$$
P^{\ast} = \min_{Q} AVC(Q)
$$

Here $AVC(Q)$ is average variable cost at output $Q$, and $P^{\ast}$ is its minimum over every feasible output, the price at which the firm just breaks even on variable cost alone. At any price below $P^{\ast}$, every unit produced costs more in variable inputs than it earns in revenue, so a firm minimising its loss produces nothing and accepts the fixed cost as a loss on its own.

## Why this node exists

A firm judged only against average total cost would shut down too early, abandoning fixed costs it has already sunk and cannot recover even by ceasing production, when continuing to produce at a smaller loss would leave it better off. The shutdown point separates that short-run decision, framed against variable cost alone, from the separate long-run question of whether the firm should have entered the market at all.
