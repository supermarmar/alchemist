## Definition

The quantity theory of money states that the money supply, multiplied by how often
each unit changes hands in a given period, equals nominal spending, the price level
multiplied by real output. How a rise in the money supply divides between higher
output and higher prices depends on how close the economy already sits to full
capacity.

## The expression

$$
M V = P Y
$$

Here $M$ is the money supply, $V$ is the velocity of circulation, $P$ is the price
level, and $Y$ is real output.

## Why this node exists

Aggregate demand shifts outward whenever the money supply rises faster than money
demand, and this identity is what fixes where that extra spending has to land.
Holding $V$ roughly constant, an economy operating below capacity absorbs the rise
mostly in $Y$, and an economy already at capacity absorbs it mostly in $P$, which is
the mechanism separating a monetary expansion that raises growth from one that only
raises inflation.
