## Definition

A market's demand curve gives the quantity buyers wish to purchase at each price, and its
supply curve gives the quantity sellers wish to offer at the same price. Equilibrium is the
single price at which those two quantities coincide.

## The expression

$$
Q_d(P^*) = Q_s(P^*)
$$

Here $Q_d(P)$ is the quantity demanded at price $P$, $Q_s(P)$ is the quantity supplied at
price $P$, and $P^*$ is the equilibrium price at which demand and supply agree.

## Why this node exists

A shift in any factor that moves demand or supply displaces this equilibrium, and a market
with no equilibrium condition has no fixed point to displace from. Elasticity of demand and
supply needs it next, since elasticity measures how far the equilibrium moves for a given
shift, not whether an equilibrium exists in the first place.
