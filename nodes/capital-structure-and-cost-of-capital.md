## Definition

Capital structure is the mix of debt and equity a corporate entity uses to finance itself, and
Modigliani and Miller's second proposition states how that mix determines the return equity
holders require once corporate borrowing exists.

## The expression

$$
R_e = R_a + (R_a - R_d) \frac{D}{E}
$$

Here $R_e$ is the required return on equity, $R_a$ is the return required on the firm's assets
as if it were financed entirely by equity, $R_d$ is the return required on its debt, and $D/E$
is the ratio of debt to equity in its capital structure. As leverage rises, equity holders
demand a higher return to compensate for the extra financial risk debt imposes on their
residual claim.

## Why this node exists

A firm's overall cost of capital cannot be found without first knowing how leverage affects
what each source of finance demands individually, since averaging the wrong required returns
together gives the wrong overall figure. Without this relationship, a firm changing its
gearing would have no way to say how that change feeds through to what its shareholders
require.
