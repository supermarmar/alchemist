## Definition

Aggregate demand and aggregate supply together determine an economy's price level and total
output: aggregate demand is the total spending planned at each price level, and aggregate
supply is the total output firms plan to produce at each price level.

## The expression

$$
AD = C + I + G + NX
$$

Here $AD$ is aggregate demand, $C$ is household consumption, $I$ is investment, $G$ is
government spending, and $NX$ is net exports, the same $X$ (exports) minus $M$ (imports) that
macroeconomic measurement sums into gross domestic product (GDP). The economy settles at the
price level and output where aggregate demand and aggregate supply intersect.

## Why this node exists

A GDP figure on its own says nothing about why output moved or what happens next if spending or
production shifts, and the aggregate demand and supply framework supplies that mechanism by
setting planned spending against planned output. The aggregate expenditure model needs it next,
since it sharpens the demand side into the specific equilibrium condition that determines
national income.
