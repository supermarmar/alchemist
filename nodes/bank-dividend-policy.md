## Definition

A bank's dividend policy sets how much of its earnings it distributes to shareholders
against how much it retains to build the capital buffer regulation and its own growth
plans require.

## The expression

$$
\text{payout ratio} = \frac{\text{dividends}}{\text{earnings}}
$$

Here dividends is the amount distributed to shareholders over the period and earnings is
the profit attributable to them over the same period, so the payout ratio states the share
of profit paid out rather than retained. A ratio near one leaves little of the period's
profit to build capital, and a supervisor may cap it directly when a bank's capital
position is under stress.

## Why this node exists

Capital retained through a lower payout ratio is capital available to absorb future
losses, so a bank's dividend decision is also a capital decision, whichever board committee
signs it off. What a bank pays its shareholders is therefore one of the figures its
stakeholders, shareholders and regulators alike, watch most closely.
