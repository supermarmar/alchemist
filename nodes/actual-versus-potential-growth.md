## Definition

Potential growth is the rate at which an economy's output could expand if all its
resources were fully employed, and actual growth is the rate it achieves; the gap between
the two, expressed as a share of potential output, is the output gap.

## The expression

$$
\text{output gap} = \frac{Y - Y^{*}}{Y^{*}}
$$

Here $Y$ is actual output, $Y^{*}$ is potential output, and the output gap is negative
when the economy runs below capacity and positive when it runs above it. A negative gap
signals spare capacity in labour and capital that inflation is not yet responding to, and
a positive gap signals the opposite.

## Why this node exists

A central bank setting policy from the growth rate alone cannot tell whether the economy
is catching up to capacity or overheating past it, since the same growth figure means
different things either side of potential. The output gap supplies that reading, and it is
the quantity the determinants of economic growth trace back to their sources.
