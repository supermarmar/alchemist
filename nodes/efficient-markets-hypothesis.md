## Definition

The efficient markets hypothesis states that a price already reflects a stated set of
available information, so that price is the market's best forecast of its own future value
given that information.

## The expression

$$
E[P_{t+1} \mid \Omega_t] = (1 + r)\, P_t
$$

Here $P_t$ is the asset price at time $t$, $\Omega_t$ is the information set assumed to be
reflected in prices, $r$ is the required rate of return, and $E[\cdot \mid \Omega_t]$ is the
expectation conditional on that information.

## Why this node exists

Weak, semi-strong and strong forms of the hypothesis differ only in what $\Omega_t$ is taken to
contain, from past prices alone through to every piece of information, public or private. The
empirical evidence for and against each form is therefore a question of how large a systematic
profit can be earned by trading on information beyond what the assumed $\Omega_t$ already
contains.
