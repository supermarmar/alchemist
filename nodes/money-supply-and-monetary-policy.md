## Definition

The money supply is the total stock of money circulating in an economy, and it exceeds the
monetary base a central bank creates because commercial banks re-lend a fraction of every
deposit they receive, generating a new deposit each time a loan is spent and redeposited.

## The expression

$$
m = \frac{1}{rr}
$$

Here $m$ is the money multiplier, the factor by which a unit of the monetary base is expanded
into broad money, and $rr$ is the reserve ratio, the fraction of each deposit a bank holds back
rather than lending on. A reserve ratio of ten per cent gives a multiplier of ten, so every euro
the central bank injects can support up to ten euro of deposits once the lending and
redepositing cycle has run its course.

## Why this node exists

Without a multiplier linking bank lending to the stock of money, a change in the monetary base
would say nothing about how much money actually circulates, and the central bank's policy
tools would have no traceable route to spending and prices. The central bank needs this node
next, since it is the mechanism through which the rate a central bank sets, or the reserves it
supplies, reaches the money supply the wider economy holds.
