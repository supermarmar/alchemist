## Definition

Capital allocation distributes a bank's finite capital across its business lines according to
the risk each line carries and the return it generates, so that a line consuming a
disproportionate share of capital is held to a correspondingly higher return.

## The expression

$$
\text{RAROC} = \frac{\text{expected income} - \text{expected loss}}{\text{economic capital}}
$$

Here expected income is the business line's revenue net of operating costs, expected loss is
the loss the line's risk profile implies on average, and economic capital is the capital the
line is allocated to absorb unexpected loss beyond that average. A line whose RAROC falls
below the bank's hurdle rate is either repriced, shrunk or has its capital allocation
withdrawn.

## Why this node exists

A bank's risk appetite sets an overall limit on how much risk it will run, but that limit says
nothing about which business line should be allowed to use how much of it, and capital
allocation is the step that turns an aggregate limit into a line-by-line entitlement. Capital
performance metrics need an allocation settled before they can measure whether a line's
return covers its cost of capital.
