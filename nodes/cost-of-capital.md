## Definition

The cost of capital is the return a company must offer its providers of debt and equity
finance to attract and retain their funding, blended into a single weighted average for use
in appraising investment projects.

## The expression

$$
\mathrm{WACC} = \frac{E}{V} R_e + \frac{D}{V} R_d (1 - T_c)
$$

Here $E$ and $D$ are the market values of equity and debt, $V = E + D$ is the total value of
the firm's financing, $R_e$ and $R_d$ are the required returns on equity and debt
respectively, and $T_c$ is the corporate tax rate, which discounts the cost of debt because
interest is tax deductible. The weighted average cost of capital is the discount rate a
project of the firm's own average risk should be appraised against.

## Why this node exists

Discounting a project's cash flows needs a single rate, but a firm is financed from more than
one source and each source demands a different return, so that single rate has to be built by
blending them rather than picking either in isolation. Capital structure and dividend policy
needs a weighted average settled here before it can ask how changing the financing mix, or the
dividend paid out of it, feeds back into the rate itself.
