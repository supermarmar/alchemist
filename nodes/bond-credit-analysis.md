## Definition

Bond credit analysis assesses a bond issuer's capacity to meet its interest and repayment
obligations, an assessment that a credit rating agency then condenses into a published rating
grade for the market to price from.

## The expression

$$
\text{interest cover} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{interest expense}}
$$

Here EBIT is the issuer's earnings before interest and tax, and interest expense is the
interest due on its outstanding debt over the same period. A low interest cover signals that
a downturn in earnings could leave the issuer unable to service its coupon, which is exactly
the deterioration a rating agency's own analysts track alongside gearing and cash flow
measures when they set or review a rating grade.

## Why this node exists

A bond's price already carries the market's own view of default risk, but that view is only
as good as the credit analysis behind it, and a rating agency exists precisely because most
investors cannot repeat that analysis for every issuer they hold. Without a credible
assessment standing behind the rating, the yield spread a corporate bond trades at would have
nothing but sentiment to anchor it.
