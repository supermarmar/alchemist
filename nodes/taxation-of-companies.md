## Definition

Taxation of companies is the charge a government levies on a company's profits, computed on a
taxable profit figure that need not equal the profit reported in its financial statements.

## The expression

$$
T = t \times P_{\text{tax}}, \qquad P_{\text{after}} = P_{\text{before}} - T
$$

Here $P_{\text{tax}}$ is taxable profit, computed by adjusting accounting profit for items the
tax authority treats differently, such as disallowed expenses and capital allowances in place
of depreciation, $t$ is the applicable tax rate, $T$ is the resulting tax charge, and
$P_{\text{after}}$ is profit after tax, the figure a shareholder's return is ultimately
measured against.

## Why this node exists

A set of financial statements cannot show what is left for shareholders, or what a company can
lawfully distribute as a dividend, until the tax charge on its profits has been calculated and
deducted, and that calculation runs on a taxable profit figure a reader cannot simply read off
the accounting profit line. Every subsequent statement of a company's after-tax performance, and
every valuation built on it, depends on this adjustment having been made correctly first.
