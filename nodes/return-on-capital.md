## Definition

Return on capital measures investment performance as the return an activity
generates relative to the capital committed to earn it. Two activities returning
the same absolute profit can carry very different returns on capital where one
needed far less capital to support it.

## The expression

$$
\text{RoC} = \frac{\pi}{K}
$$

Here $\pi$ is the return, typically profit or economic value added over the
period, $K$ is the capital committed over the same period, and $\text{RoC}$ is the
resulting return on capital. Where $K$ is regulatory capital instead of economic
capital, the same ratio is often labelled return on regulatory capital, and the
two need not agree.

## Why this node exists

A business line's absolute return says nothing about how efficiently it used the
capital it was allocated, and a return on capital figure is what lets two lines of
very different size be compared on equal terms. It also gives a board the metric
against which a hurdle rate is set, so an activity is funded only where its return
on capital clears the cost of the capital backing it.
