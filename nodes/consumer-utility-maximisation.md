## Definition

A consumer maximises utility by choosing, from every bundle of goods the budget line makes
affordable, the one that reaches the highest indifference curve, and that choice sits where
the two curves are tangent to each other.

## The expression

$$
\frac{MU_x}{MU_y} = \frac{P_x}{P_y}
$$

Here $MU_x$ and $MU_y$ are the marginal utilities of goods $x$ and $y$, the extra utility one
more unit of each good provides, and $P_x$ and $P_y$ are their prices. The ratio on the left is
the marginal rate of substitution, the rate at which the consumer is willing to trade one good
for the other while staying equally satisfied, and utility is maximised where that rate equals
the price ratio the market actually offers.

## Why this node exists

Demand and supply analysis takes a consumer's willingness to buy as given, but that
willingness itself follows from a consumer choosing the best bundle a fixed budget allows, and
this condition is what fixes which bundle that is. Behavioural economics needs this rational
benchmark settled first, since it is defined by the systematic ways real choices depart from
it.
