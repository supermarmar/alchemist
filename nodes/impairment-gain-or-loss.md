The change in an entity's loss allowance from one reporting date to the next is recognised in profit or loss as an impairment loss where the allowance increases and as an impairment gain where it decreases, including a decrease below the credit losses originally expected at initial recognition. This is what links a bank's period-on-period movement in expected credit loss provisions to its income statement, and the same movement in provisions also changes the balance sheet carrying value of the exposure and, ultimately, the bank's capital position.
