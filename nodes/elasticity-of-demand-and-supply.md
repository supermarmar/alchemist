## Definition

An elasticity measures the proportionate change in the quantity demanded or supplied that
follows a proportionate change in price or income. Demand elasticity is conventionally
negative, since quantity demanded falls as price rises, and supply elasticity is
conventionally positive; a value near zero marks a quantity that barely moves, and a large
value marks one that moves sharply.

## The expression

$$
E = \frac{(Q_2 - Q_1) \big/ \left(\frac{Q_1+Q_2}{2}\right)}{(P_2 - P_1) \big/ \left(\frac{P_1+P_2}{2}\right)}
$$

Here $E$ is the arc elasticity, $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ are the quantities observed before and
after the price change, and $P_1$ and $P_2$ are the prices before and after. Taking the
average of each pair as the base gives the same value whichever direction the price
moves, where an endpoint base would give two different answers for the same movement.
The identical formula, with $Q$ read as quantity supplied, gives the elasticity of supply.

## Why this node exists

A verbal label such as "price sensitive" carries no number a forecast can be built on, and
this node supplies the number: how far revenue moves after a price change, and how the
burden of a tax splits between buyers and sellers, both depend on the size of the
elasticity involved. The availability of substitutes, the time allowed for the market to adjust and
where on the curve the change occurs all move the value, so the same good can be measured
as elastic over one range and inelastic over another.
