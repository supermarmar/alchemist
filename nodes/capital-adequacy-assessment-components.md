## Definition

A bank's assessment of its capital adequacy under its base case outlook brings together its
financial forecast, including capital available, against the Pillar 1 minimum requirement, the
Pillar 2 add-on, and any regulatory buffers sitting on top of both.

## The expression

$$
\text{Capital surplus} = A - \bigl(P_1 + P_{2A} + B\bigr)
$$

Here $A$ is capital available under the forecast, $P_1$ is the Pillar 1 minimum capital
requirement, $P_{2A}$ is the firm-specific Pillar 2A add-on set by the supervisor, and $B$ is
the sum of the regulatory buffers, such as the capital conservation buffer and any
countercyclical buffer, that must be held in addition. A positive result shows the forecast
holds enough capital to meet every layer of the requirement at once; a negative one signals a
capital shortfall the assessment must address.

## Why this node exists

Comparing forecast capital against the Pillar 1 minimum alone would understate what the firm
actually has to hold, since Pillar 2A and the buffers are binding requirements in their own
right, and not optional cushions. Material integrated risk assessment needs this
decomposition next, because it is only once each component of the requirement is separated out
that the firm can trace a capital shortfall back to the specific risk driving it.
