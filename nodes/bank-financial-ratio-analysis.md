## Definition

Financial ratio analysis compares a bank's performance and financial strength against
peers by computing ratios from its income statement and balance sheet; return on equity
is the ratio most often used to summarise overall profitability.

## The expression

$$
ROE = \frac{\text{Net income}}{\text{Shareholders' equity}}
$$

Here $ROE$ is return on equity, net income is the bank's profit after tax for the period,
and shareholders' equity is the book value of the capital its shareholders have
contributed and retained. A bank can raise $ROE$ either by earning more net income for
the same equity base or by shrinking that equity base, which is why the ratio is read
alongside a leverage or capital measure.

## Why this node exists

A bank's raw income statement and balance sheet figures are not comparable across
institutions of different sizes, and ratios such as return on equity are what restate
those figures on a common footing so that one bank's profitability can be set against
another's. Where a peer comparison shows one bank's ratio moving out of line with the
rest of its sector, the consequence is a closer look at what is driving it, capital
structure, cost control or asset quality, before the figure is taken at face value.
